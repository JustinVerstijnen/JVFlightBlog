> Lesson 2.10 - Precautionary landing
> Date: 21-09-2026

## Introduction

In this lesson, we performed the Precautionary Landing exercise. This is a separate exercise from the other lessons we did till now, and focusses on to land the aircraft in a moderate controlled way outside of any aerodrome but in a field. As we still have our engine, primary and secondary flight controls, we are less dependent on those critical factors.

Causes of you having to perform a precautionary landing are often to save lives without any aerodrome in range but still having an engine and flight controls:

- Fuel miscalculation or suspected fuel leak
- Sick passenger on board
- End of daylight period and/or planned aerodrome(s) closed
- Lost in the vicinity, no contact with GPS, other aircraft
- Unplanned weather deterioration, or getting not suitable for VFR conditions

## The exercise explained

So the Precautionary landing exercise is somewhat different from the Forced Landing exercise, as we still have our engine and our primary/secondary flight controls. As we have this, we can make a landing in a much safer way than just putting it what is within our gliding range and wants us to first do an inspection run of the chosen field.

During the inspection run, we will fly at a constant speed of around 70 knots and count how long it takes from start to end of the field. This tells us by a rule of thumb how long the field is so we can decide to land there. We check the following things during this run:

- Time from start to end
- Trees, power lines
- Field material (softness, condition)
- Cattle/animals
- Objects at the start and end of the field
- Other possible risks

{{% alert title="Info" color="info" %}}
As we can count the time from start to end at around 500ft, we can use this rule of thumb to get an indication (not exact) of the available landing distance:

_Indicated airspeed : 2 x seconds = distance traveled_

__

This means, traveling 100 knots in 10 seconds is 500 meters, 62 knots in 20 seconds is 620 meters. The amount of knots devided by 2 is meters per second.

So we fly 2 circles around the field, first to inspect and then to prepare and land like a normal circuit. Then we perform a soft field landing. On the last downwind, we could make use of our radio capability to inform the tower or flight information services that we need medical assistance. This can result in having that assistance minutes earlier.

## Remarks

So we have performed this exercise around 3 times in this lesson. It went not perfect but also not bad. I chose the right fields to land on, preferably into the wind which gives us less ground roll. The first time, I was not perfect on speed with flaps 20 and with 10 knots of wind so I was corrected. The second and third time were much better and the third time actually having a good approach to the field with good speed.

After 3 times, we headed back to the airfield for 3 circuits with crosswind with okay landings but crosswind flying and landings are a good training area as this requires much more precise flying.
