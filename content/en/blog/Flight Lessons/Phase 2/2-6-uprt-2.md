## Flight lesson 2.6 - UPRT 2

| Lesson number | Lesson name | Lesson date(s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2.6 | UPRT 2 | 20-11-2026 |

The flight lesson 2.6 with name UPRT 2 is an advanced flight lesson where we learn some Upset Prevention and Recovery Training (UPRT). This means we will be trained to recognize the plane getting in a spin or spiral dive and be able to recover successfully. Getting in those situations can happen, and be better prepared and know what to do than facing this and completely lose control.

In this lesson we did the exercises below, but its mostly an experience flight. So this means I was not graded in any way but got an introduction of how to prevent the situations done in this lesson in real flight.

- A spin
- A spiral dive
- A barrel roll
- A looping
- A break away
- A simulated bird hit where the instructor opened the canopy

## Weather

the weather was great during this lesson. Some light clouds on about 1.100 feet but mostly transparent so nothing to worry about. The METAR of a close aerodrome stated:

`METAR XXXX 201255Z AUTO 03004KT 300V100 9999 NCD 07/03 Q1002=`

## Starting up

Before we got into the plane, I got a briefing about the lesson program and what I could expect from it. We went through the weather, NOTAMs and possible risks and walked through the exercises.

As I also got to fly a new aircraft type, the Fuji 200 (SUBA), I also got a quick rundown on the differences between that and a Cessna 172. It got some different properties like a low-wing, left/right fuel selector (no both), fuel booster pump and a canopy which can be closed and opened before getting in/out. Also the Vspeeds are different to a Cessna 172, but a rule of thumb was to add 5 knots to every Cessna 172 Vspeed:

- Rotate at 60 knots instead of 55
- Climb with 70 knots instead of 65
- Glide with 70 knots instead of 65

Then we tanked some AVGAS to have at least 90 minutes of fuel as we plan to fly about 45 minutes.

After take-off we climbed to 5.000 feet. For me it was the first time flying that high, as the previous record was about 3.300 feet during the stalls lessons.

## Spins

After being established at 5.000 feet and out of the sunlight, we did a spin. This is a situation where we purposely fail a slow flight, approach to stall recovery and full stall recovery by being in a full stall and press left rudder fully. The plane will then enter a spin.

The recovery procedure is:

1. Close throttle
2. Opposite rudder
3. Transition to the climb setting
4. Add power below 100 knots

We did this spin twice to get the hang of it. The recovery procedure is burned into my memory but at the moment you will be not that fast at acting, as it goes so fast and you feel a lot of G forces. The second spin I was better prepared and that recovery went good.

The vertical speed is around minus 500 feet per minute during a spin.

## Spiral dive

After the spin we did a spiral dive. This is when we spin and we also fail to recover from that, making our speed increase and increase with a different recover procedure.

The recovery procedure is:

1. Close throttle
2. Roll straight with the ailerons
3. Transition to the climb setting
4. Add power below 100 knots

The vertical speed is around minus 1.000 feet per minute during a spiral dive, increasing as we don't recover.

Both of these exercises we let the plane do 2 rotations before recovering, to know which side is the opposite side and the correct recovery procedure.

## Barrel roll

After the spins and spiral we did two barrel rolls, where we fly inverted for the first time. One over left and one over right.

## Looping

After the barrel rolls we did two loopings, where we hit the highest amount of G forces. This is a cool experience wherewe first see ground, then only air, and then ground and then the normal picture again.

We dived to hit 130 knots, then we cut the power and did the looping. During the end of the looping you can get some buffet (stall warning) as the angle of attack is too high at that point.

## Break away

I did not fly this exercise myself, this was fully done my my instructor for a nearby passenger flight. He is trained to do formation flying so did a little present for a passenger flight of the same company. Lining up next to a Cessna 172 and breaking away over right as we are a fighter jet.

## Simulated bird strike

After completing all of the exercises we went back to the aerodrome and simulated a bird strike by opening the canopy. Then the instructor said, So Justin, land this thing.

This is a completely new experience as you fly at around 80 knots (~ 135 km/h) with a lot of air coming around you, so we had to get our face behind the windshield. It was around 3 degrees outside so it was a little cold.

I did the circuit just like always and tried to do a great landing. The landing was a bit hard but we were on the ground. The instructor told me that its a great landing when in an emergency like this but expects better in normal conditions. Haha.

## Summary

So I had no real remarks as I flew on this aircraft type for the first time, and had a simulated emergency. It was a cool lesson where I had to do much but it was also a lot of my instructor showing things. This is one of the most rememberable lessons till now.

Also a lot of records, I hit the highest ground speed when flying myself, the highest altitude and the highest amount of G-force with +4,39 G. I left a little dizzy after the lesson but it was totally worth it.

Next time its back on a normal Cessna 172.
