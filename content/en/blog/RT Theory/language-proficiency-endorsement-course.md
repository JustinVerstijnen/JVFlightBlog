## Language Proficiency Endorsement course overview

The Language Proficiency Endorsement course basically a test of your proficiency in speaking English. This test will be done by a practioned examinor which gives you a score. A maximum of 600 points can be achieved. The assessment includes you to be able to speak, listen and conversate in English in a good and fluent manner:

| Assessment component | Score |
| --- | --- |
| Naming basic items on an instrument panel | Normal |
| Explaining basic aviation terminology, such as height, elevation, attitude, yaw and apron | Normal |
| Naming basic parts of an aircraft, such as flaps, beacon light, spinner and nose wheel | Normal |
| Discussing an aviation-related subject | Normal |
| Pronouncing abbreviations found in NOTAMs in full | Normal |
| Listening assessment | Important |
| Fluency assessment | Most important |

Most points are awarded for the listening and fluency sections. The fluency section counts twice as heavy as the listening section and is described as the most important section of the assessment.

The fluency preparation consists of:

- A list of useful phrases.
- A list of non-standard situations in which the candidate must respond to ATC in English.

After performing the endorsement, a score is built up where a total of 6 different levels can be achieved:

| LPE level | Validity |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Not specified |
| 2 | Not specified |
| 3 | Not specified |
| 4 | 4 years |
| 5 | 6 years |
| 6 | Valid for life |

This page I used to prepare myself for the assessment and to create different example phrases and for myself to be able to construct different sentences.

## Module 2: Interactive exercise at Malaga Airport

In this module, I was given a example of a situation that can happen. The main area's are to understand what is happening between ATC and the pilots of another plane and why various actions are done or wrong.

{{% alert title="Situation example:" color="info" %}}
A pilot was in a private Cessna on the apron at Malaga Airport. The pilot contacted Ground on 121.70 and requested departure clearance. Clearance was received and the pilot was directed to runway 14.The pilot then requested permission from Tower to taxi to runway 14 via intersection C-2. Permission was received. At intersection C-2, holding point runway 14, the pilot completed the engine check, or run-up, and then called Tower for take-off clearance using the phrase “Ready for Departure.”Tower did not answer. The pilot repeated the call, but again received no answer. The pilot then tried to contact Ground and Approach, but neither station answered. The pilot concluded that the radio was out of order.Because the airport was busy, the pilot could not simply taxi across it. The pilot called the aeroclub using a mobile phone and obtained the telephone number of the Tower. After explaining the situation to the Tower, the aircraft was back on the apron approximately fifteen minutes later. The radio was repaired and the pilot safely reached Granada Airport later that day.
{{% /alert %}}

### Question 1

What do you think of the situation of the Dutch Cessna?

{{% alert title="My answer" color="info" %}}
His decision to try the radio is good. I think he just had to first set 7600 on his transponder after concluding the radio has an error.
{{% /alert %}}

### Question 2

Would you just have taxied back to the apron without contacting Tower?

{{% alert title="My answer" color="info" %}}
No, I would have been there until I got an specific clearance to taxi from one of the ATC organs.
{{% /alert %}}

### Question 3

What if the pilot had not known the telephone number of the aeroclub?

{{% alert title="My answer" color="info" %}}
7600 on the transponder and look at the tower for light signals or he could just Google it or call the national aviation authority to transfer to the tower control.
{{% /alert %}}

### Question 4

Would the situation have been different at a very small airfield?

{{% alert title="My answer" color="info" %}}
Yes, if the airfield is uncontrolled we don't need to be cleared for taxi/landing/departure and we could simply turn around while using visual references.
{{% /alert %}}

### Question 5

What kind of directions or actions will Tower have given or undertaken?

{{% alert title="My answer" color="info" %}}
I think they will give a taxi-back clearance or a number/method of reaching ground to get a taxi-back clearance or shooting light signals from the tower.
{{% /alert %}}

| --- | --- | --- |

| 3 | This is the PH-VFC. I have a problem; one of my passengers ... | This is the PH-VFC. I have a problem; one of my passengers feels sick and needs medical assistance, requesting immediate landing. |
| 4 | I understand that ... Does that influence the normal landing procedures? | I understand that helicoper medical emergency services are active. Does that influence the normal landing procedures? |
| 5 | I need first aid directly after landing ... | I need first aid directly after landing on runway 29, requesting immediate taxi clearance. |
| 6 | It is unclear whether ... | It is unclear whether my oil pressure indicator is faulty or we have an actual problem building up. |
| 7 | Can you give some extra information on ...? | Can you give some extra information on the facilities on your aerodrome? |

| 9 | Can you tell me why ...? | Can you tell me why runway 04 is closed today? |
| 10 | Can you tell me what the cause is of ...? | Can you tell me what the cause is of the helicopter departing straight ahead? |
| 11 | After landing I would like someone to check ... | After landing I would like someone to check my backseat passenger who needs medical assistance. |
| 12 | I notice that ... | I notice that an aircraft part, I think a nose wheel, is lying on the runway |

## Module 4: Non-standard fluency situations

For each situation, my given task is to say a proper/required response to ATC. My objective material does not provide answers, so the answer is worked out by myself. The answer contains the sentences/sayings I have to perform where I have to keep the Radio Telephony rules in mind. I also use my callsign PH-JSV as example.

### Situation 4.1: Ground movement incident at Hilversum

An ambulance is reported to be positioned close to the runway.

Your response must:

- Ask how far the ambulance is from the runway.
- Ask how long the situation is expected to last.
- Ask whether it affects the normal landing procedure.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Hilversum Radio, PH-JSV, I see from Base that there is an ambulance close to the runway. Do you know how far this ambulance is away from the runway, how long this takes and if this effects my landing procedure?
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.2: Passenger requiring first aid at Eindhoven

A passenger in the back is a little scared of flying and after getting into the plane, the anxiety increased resulting in hyperventilation and transpiring.

Your response must:

- State that you are aborting the departure procedure immediately.
- Report that there is a problem.
- Explain that one of your passengers has heart problems.
- Ask whether medical assistance can be arranged.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Eindhoven Ground, PH-JSV, aborting current flight plan due of a sick passenger. One of my passengers with some heart problems is hyperventilating. Requesting taxi clearance back to the apron and requesting some medical assistance.
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.3: Unfamiliar changes at Paderborn

You landed at this German airfield two years ago, but it now looks very different from then.

Your response must:

- State that you landed at the airfield two years earlier.
- Explain that the airfield now looks different.
- State that this is very confusing.
- Ask whether changes were made very recently.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Paderborn Ground, PH-JSV, eeh I landed here about 2 years ago but it looks very different and confusing. Can you tell me if the aerodrome changed recently and can you help me find the way to the general aviation apron?
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.4: Temporary pilot disability at Lelystad

Your response must:

- Report that there is a problem.
- State that you have lost all feeling in your right leg.
- Explain that the only person beside you is a student pilot.
- State that the student pilot has only eight flying hours.
- Say that you want to monitor how the situation develops.
- State that you will make contact again in ten minutes.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Lelystad Tower, PH-JSV, Flying downwind, Problem here, I have lost feeling in my right leg and flying with student who has 8 flight hours. We will perform a go around and monitor how this situation develops. Expect student landing, reporting back in 10 minutes
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.5: Helicopters near a cycling race at Eelde

You read about a cycling race in the NOTAMs. Following a major crash, the race will pass near Eelde much later than planned.

Your response must:

- Request information about the position of the helicopters associated with the cycling race.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Eelde Approach, PH-JSV, foudn out about the cycling race near the airport through NOTAMs. Requesting location of the race and the position of the helicopters.
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.6: Possible fuel leak near Seppe

You are flying to Ostend and you appear to be losing fuel.

Your response must:

- State that you are flying to Ostend.
- Report that you appear to be losing fuel.
- State that the cause is unknown.
- Explain that you are monitoring the situation closely.
- State that you will make contact again within ten minutes.
- State that you will probably make an intermediate landing at Seppe.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Seppe Radio, PH-JSV, en route to Oostende, dicovered a possible fuel leak with an unknown cause. Monitoring situation closely, reporting back in 10 minutes. Expect intermediate landing at Seppe.
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.7: Thunderstorm approaching Rodez

It is a warm summer day. A thunderstorm is approaching from the east at approximately 50 NM. You are ready for departure and waiting for permission to depart.

Your response must:

- State that your destination is Bordeaux.
- Explain that Bordeaux is directly to the west.
- State that you would like to depart as soon as possible.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Rodez Tower, PH-JSV, Ready for departure to Bordeaux to the west. Request immediate departure due to the approaching thunderstorm.
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.8: Missed approach at Den Helder

You were confused by an aircraft that appeared to be preparing for take-off. You can now see that it is taxiing away from the runway.

Your response must:

- Explain what caused the confusion.
- State that you can now see the aircraft taxiing away from the runway.
- State that you will perform a go-around.
- State that the go-around will be followed by a second landing attempt.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
De Kooy Tower, PH-JSV, Going around due to traffic on the runway, appears to have aborted their take-off as it taxies away from the runway. Approaching again in 5 minutes.
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.9: Aircraft behaving abnormally at Texel

Your aircraft is behaving differently from normal. You had already announced your intention to land at Texel.

Your response must:

- State that the aircraft is behaving differently from normal.
- State that you will not continue with the announced landing at Texel.
- State that you will continue to Den Helder Airport.
- Explain that you know Den Helder Airport well.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Texel Radio, PH-JSV, Aborting landing intention at Texel. It is unclear whether it is the elevator or something with the flaperons. Diverting to De Kooy airport which is better known by me.
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.10: Advertising balloon at Hamburg

You can see a balloon from a considerable distance.

Your response must ask for confirmation:

- That it is an advertising balloon.
- That it is secured to the ground.
- That it therefore cannot and will not take off.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Hamburg Tower, PH-JSV, On downwind, Can you confirm that the balloon is secured to the ground and can you confirm it cannot take-off?
{{% /alert %}}

### Situation 4.11: Forest fire over the Veluwe

When flying over the beautiful Veluwe, you see a big forest fire with no emergency services already at the location.

The supplied document states:

- Call for Firefighting services
- Call for Possible emergency services

No further task or specific question is included in the supplied document.

{{% alert title="My answer:" color="info" %}}
Dutch Mill Info, PH-JSV. Flying overhead the Veluwe. I want to report a big forest fire directly under my location with big chunks of smoke. Can you establish some emergency services?
{{% /alert %}}
