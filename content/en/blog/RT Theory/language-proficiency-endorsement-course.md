## Language Proficiency Endorsement course overview

The Language Proficiency Endorsement course basically a test of your proficiency in speaking English. This test will be done by a practioned examinor which gives you a score. A maximum of 600 points can be achieved. The assessment includes you to be able to speak, listen and conversate in English in a good and fluent manner:

| Assessment component | Score |
| --- | --- |
| Naming basic items on an instrument panel | Normal |
| Explaining basic aviation terminology, such as height, elevation, attitude, yaw and apron | Normal |
| Naming basic parts of an aircraft, such as flaps, beacon light, spinner and nose wheel | Normal |
| Discussing an aviation-related subject | Normal |
| Pronouncing abbreviations found in NOTAMs in full | Normal |
| Listening assessment | Important |
| Fluency assessment | Most important |

Most points are awarded for the listening and fluency sections. The fluency section counts twice as heavy as the listening section and is described as the most important section of the assessment.

The fluency preparation consists of:

- A list of useful phrases.
- A list of non-standard situations in which the candidate must respond to ATC in English.

After performing the endorsement, a score is built up where a total of 6 different levels can be achieved:

| LPE level | Validity |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Not specified |
| 2 | Not specified |
| 3 | Not specified |
| 4 | 4 years |
| 5 | 6 years |
| 6 | Valid for life |

This page I used to prepare myself for the assessment and to create different example phrases and for myself to be able to construct different sentences.

## Module 2: Interaction exercise at Malaga Airport

In this module, I was given a example of a situation that can happen. The main area's are to understand what is happening between ATC and the pilots of another plane and why various actions are done or wrong. The example of the situation is:

A pilot was in a private Cessna on the apron at Malaga Airport. The pilot contacted Ground on 121.70 and requested departure clearance. Clearance was received and the pilot was directed to runway 14.

The pilot then requested permission from Tower to taxi to runway 14 via intersection C-2. Permission was received. At intersection C-2, holding point runway 14, the pilot completed the engine check, or run-up, and then called Tower for take-off clearance using the phrase “Ready for Departure.”

Tower did not answer. The pilot repeated the call, but again received no answer. The pilot then tried to contact Ground and Approach, but neither station answered. The pilot concluded that the radio was out of order.

Because the airport was busy, the pilot could not simply taxi across it. The pilot called the aeroclub using a mobile phone and obtained the telephone number of the Tower. After explaining the situation to the Tower, the aircraft was back on the apron approximately fifteen minutes later. The radio was repaired and the pilot safely reached Granada Airport later that day.
